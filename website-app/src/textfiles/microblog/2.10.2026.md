Lately, I've been thinking about how important language is, and specifically the dialect. I am one of quite few in Finland who speak Finnish-Swedish. No, it's not Swedish. With my language, Swedes don't neccessarily understand me and I think it's because of two things. For one, we shorten a lot of our words, both in speech and in text, which makes us incredibly efficient in producing content. And secondly, we speak more monotonously, like really monotonously. To our ears, Swedish from Sweden, can sound forced and sometimes passive-aggressive. It feels like someone is talking about something while the underlying point is something else. And the small-talk is something Swedes do a lot apparently, despite them saying they don't. Kinda like a passive-aggressive customer-service voice.

My point was that I don't neccessarily feel at home anywhere. For example, I am legally supposed to have access to public services in Finnish-Swedish in Finland, but it's never the case. It's especially stressing me out about having an emergency where I'm scared shitless about myself or someone else, and I can't communicate with the emergency personnel properly. I know this issue isn't specific to Finland, but also an experience in other countries with multiple national languages, so I do believe some of you can relate to the feeling of not really being comfortable or feeling at home anywhere. In my case, I feel like Finnish-Swedish is a special case since I can't even go to Sweden to speak my language since they don't really understand me when my language is so different with different words, cadence, and pronounciations.
