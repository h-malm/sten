Lately, I've been thinking about how important my language is to me, and even more important is my dialect. I am one of quite few in Finland who belong to the Swedish-speaking-communities. No, we are not Swedish. I am Finnish, but my mothertongue is Finland-Swedish, and it is not the same as Swedish. Believe me, I wish it was like Swedish, because then I'd at least be understood if I travelled to Sweden, but with my language, they don't neccessarily understand us and I think it's because of two things. For one, we shorten a lot of our words, both in speech and in text, which makes us incredibly efficient in producing content. And secondly, we speak more monotonously, like really monotonously. To our ears, Swedish from Sweden, not only Stockholm, sounds quite fake and obnoxious. It feels like they are talking about something and meaning something else. And the small-talk is something Swedes do a lot apparently, despite them saying they don't. The time I've spent in Sweden, visiting friends and relatives, is very clouded in small-talk, and the small-talk combined with the enthusiasm and melody of Swedish makes it sound like I'm being talked down to. Kinda like a passive-aggressive customer-service voice. I've noted that people in the stores in Sweden do not greet you as you walk in. Now, that is something I thought they'd be pushing for, but I've witnessed it myself. In Finland, where it is done in every single store, it makes me feel like I am allowed to be there and welcomed, so without it, I felt like I was intruding (but that might also just be anxiety clouding my judgement).

My point was that I don't neccessarily feel at home anywhere. For example, I am legally allowed to have public services in Finland-Swedish in Finland, but it's never the case, and it's especially making me stress out about having an emergency where I'm scared shitless about myself or someone else and I can't communicate with the emergency personnel properly. I know this issue isn't specific to Finland, but also an experience in other countries with multiple official languages, so I do believe some of you can relate to the feeling of not really ever being comfortable or feeling at home anywhere. In my case, I feel like Finland-Swedish is a special case since I can't even go to Sweden to feel like I can speak my language since they don't really understand me because my language is so different with different words and pronounciations.
